Redesigning how a stablecoin send starts, then testing it against the original

Sending crypto on Grey, starting with the right question

A self-initiated redesign of how a stablecoin send starts on Grey, tested against a recreation of the current flow with 11 crypto senders
Role
Solo: research, product design, prototyping, and building the test platform
Timeline
Designed early October 2026. Test platform built 8 to 10 October 2026. Sessions ran on 12 October 2026
Method
Within-subject A/B usability test, 11 participants, remote on their own phones
Headline result
9 of 11 people found the redesign easier, and its average ease rating rose from 3.8 to 5.4 out of 7
TL;DR On Grey, sending USDC to a wallet starts at a list of bank recipients, so you don't learn you're on the right path until several screens in. My redesign changes only the start: Send first asks "Where is the money going?", and choosing a crypto wallet opens Grey's own wallet form straight away. With 11 crypto senders, the redesign felt clearly easier and needed fewer back taps and misclicks. It was probably faster, but the time difference is not proven at this sample size, and one person sent on the wrong network in the redesign.

This is a self-initiated project for my portfolio. I am not affiliated with Grey, and Grey did not commission or review this work. Version A is my recreation of Grey's current flow, built from screenshots of the live app.

01

Grey treats a wallet send like a bank transfer

I started this after trying to send crypto to a friend's wallet on Grey. Tapping Send took me to my saved bank recipients, then a currency list led by Naira, and I wasn't sure I was in the right place. The process felt hectic. Grey lets people send USDC and USDT to crypto wallets, but its send flow is built around bank recipients and currencies. To send USDC to a friend's wallet, you go from Home to your USDC balance, tap Send, choose to add a new recipient, pick USDC from a list of currencies, fill in a recipient form, and then enter the amount.

Two points work against the task:

  1. It starts at a list of bank recipients, with NGN and USD filters. You have to tap "New recipient" to begin.
  2. The coin is chosen as "the currency your recipient receives", in a list led by Naira and US Dollar, with stablecoins mixed in.

Nothing tells a wallet sender they're on the right path until the wallet form appears, in a flow where Grey's own form warns that "funds cannot be reversed". My test later confirmed the first point: 8 of 11 people hesitated at the bank recipient list.

Why this matters to Grey is my inference, not something I measured. Grey says it charges nothing extra on USDC sends beyond the network fee (Grey's blog, September 2024), so a failed send doesn't cost it a fee directly. The cost is trust: someone who finds sending stablecoins confusing, or loses money to the wrong network, may move their dollars to another app, along with the conversion and withdrawal fees Grey does earn.

I also read about 280 Play Store reviews of Grey. The ones about crypto mention fees, slow deposits and support, not the send flow, so the evidence for this problem comes from my own test.

"Not typical of a common crypto decentralized exchange, especially with naira accounts appearing first." Participant, on Grey's flow
02

One question before anything else

In the redesign, Send opens a sheet that asks "Where is the money going?": "To a crypto wallet" ("Send USDT or USDC to any wallet address") or "To a bank account" ("Paid out in local currency, like GBP or NGN"). Choosing a crypto wallet opens Grey's wallet form straight away, where you pick the token and network and paste the address. A wallet send never sees a list of bank recipients or fiat currencies.

I also considered starting wallet sends only from the USDC or USDT balance screen. It was a smaller change, but anyone who taps the main Send button would still land on bank recipients. Destination first adds one tap before the first screen. I accepted that because it tells people straight away which path they're on, and it removes two screens built for bank transfers from every wallet send.

03

Changing only the start, so the test means something

My first version also redesigned the wallet form, the amount screen, and added review and receipt screens. If it had won the test, I couldn't have said which change made the difference. Before running the test with participants, I cut it back to how the send starts. The wallet form, the amount screen and everything after them stay Grey's, so any difference in the test comes from that one decision.

04

How I tested it

I built a small usability lab: both versions as clickable phone prototypes, a consent and screening page, and a tracker that records what each person does. Everyone sent $5 of USDC to a friend's wallet on BNB Smart Chain in both versions. About half started with each version: 6 with Grey's flow and 5 with the redesign. 15 people started and 11 finished both. All had sent crypto to a wallet before; all were in Nigeria, on their phones; 5 had used Grey.

Reaching the end of the flow counts as completing the task. I judged only the choices the screens ask for, the path, the token and the network, not the amount: several people sent $6.02 so their friend would receive $5.00 after the fee, which is the maths, not the flow. I set the success targets before the test: completion of 80% or more, fewer errors, no critical errors, a typical time at least 20% lower, ease at least 1 point higher, and a clear majority preferring the redesign.

05

What happened

What the test shows clearly is that asking where the money is going first made the send feel easier and cut the backtracking. By the rule I set before the test, though, this is not a full success: one guardrail failed. The one wrong-network send happened in the redesign. Its network picker is the same screen as Grey's, so the change I tested is unlikely to have caused it, but I can't rule it out.

"Typical" is the median: the middle person's result, so one very slow run doesn't skew it. "Clear difference" means a paired test on the same 11 people found it unlikely to be chance. The errors row counts finished sends only, not wrong picks people corrected along the way.

"Seeing it as an option early was really nice, didn't have to struggle to look for a place to make my transaction." Participant, on the redesign

The redesign wasn't perfect. 3 of 11 closed the new sheet or went elsewhere before choosing, and one person took 7 minutes 20 seconds on the redesign, their first version, before finding the right path. One person preferred Grey's flow: a regular Grey user who took exactly the same time on both versions. Part of the time saving is also learning: people who did Grey's flow first typically saved 37.8s on the redesign, while people who did the redesign first saved only 6.1s.

06

What I'd do differently

  • Check my reading of the live app sooner. I first noted that Grey pre-fills the network and switches to a light theme on the form. Both were wrong, and I only caught them when I looked again.
  • Make the two options on the sheet more distinct. 3 of 11 people closed it before choosing, so next I'd test clearer icons for crypto and bank, and wording like "Wallet address" against "Bank account in NGN, GBP and more".
  • Test one change at a time. Cutting my first redesign back to a single decision is what made the result mean anything, and I'll scope future tests the same way.
